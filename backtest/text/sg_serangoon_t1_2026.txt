**Term 1 School-Based Assessments**

14. Weighted Assessments (WA) refer to assessments where the scores form part of the computation of a student’s
overall results in a subject for the year. Each subject has at most one Weighted Assessment per school term. This
allows teachers to better pace out teaching and learning and enhance students’ learning experiences. In turn, this
helps students to build their confidence and develop an intrinsic motivation to learn. Weighted Assessments can
take different forms for different subjects: Class Tests, Practical Tests, Projects, Performance Tasks, Coursework,
or Assignments. The WA schedule, format of assessment and topic/content can be found in **Annex C** .

15. Students will not be given any make-up time if they report late for WAs. If students report after the end of a WA,
they will be deemed as absent for the assessment.

16. **Absence from Weighted Assessment (WA) must be covered with a Medical Certificate** with the diagnosis
stated, from a medical practitioner registered under the Medical Registration Act on the Singapore Medical Council
website. **Parents’ letters are not allowed.** Any absence during the school term for overseas travel will be recorded
as “Absent Without Valid Reason.” Please also note that if your child is absent for any weighted assessments during
this period, the assessment will be awarded a **zero mark** .

17. In the event of absence from class tests due to valid reasons, e.g. representing school or country in official
sanctioned competitions and conferences, a “Valid Reason (VR)” will be given for the class test. This is in recognition
of students’ holistic dev elopment, given that the student development and academic domains are both important.
Students can attempt the WA at a later date for the purposes of practice, and feedback will be given to support their
learning.

18. Beyond WAs, teachers also formatively assess learning outcomes, strengths, and areas for growth of each student
by employing questioning techniques during lessons and assigning class work and homework. Teachers will indicate
the assigned homework on the Homework Board in the classroom, and students should record in their copy of the
Student Handbook.

19. Students are strongly encouraged to seek consultations with teachers in other subject areas, whenever they are in
doubt. This will help to address learning gaps earlier in a timely manner.

28. **Student-Initiated Learning (SIL)** . SIL is an opportunity for your child/ward to learn to be an independent,
passionate, and lifelong learner. It enables students to exercise agency, explore their interests and passions, and
learn within and beyond the curriculum. Thus, it seeks to develop students’ intrinsic motivation and nurture self -
directed learning. It also helps students discover their interests and plan how they should go about pursuing them.

29. Sec 1 students will be signed up to go through online modules to build on their ICT baseline skills. Through these
modules, students will be equipped with a mastery of practical digital skills for the future digital workplace.

30. Sec 2 and Sec 3 students will be assigned a planning template at the start of Term 2. Your child/ward will need to
identify and indicate his or her interest under 5 themes identified for SIL. These 5 themes (Knowing Asia, The Arts,
Science-Technology-Engineering-Mathematics (S.T.E.M.), Entrepreneurship, and Overall Well-Being and Life Skills)
are intended to encompass a broad range of activities so as to better appeal to the diverse learning interests and
needs of all students. Sec 2 to Sec 3 students are encouraged to continue with the plan that they had started in the
previous year to deepen their understanding of and interest in their previous activity. Your child/ward’s SIL plans
are to be submitted to ensure that they are appropriate and safe to conduct at home. Students are reminded to
exercise care and caution in carrying out the selected activity to keep themselves and those around them safe.

31. Sec 4 and Sec 5 students will focus on Education and Career Guidance (ECG) where they can make use of this
dedicated time and space for them to do research on their post-secondary pathways and take ownership of their
future.

32. Your child/ward will not be graded based on what he or she has learnt during SIL. This is to motivate students’
learning and avoid an over-emphasis on grades, particularly in this context of harnessing their interests to nurture
intrinsic motivation. At the end of their SIL journey in Term 4 (for Sec 1 to 3 students) or Term 2 (for Sec 4 and 5
students), students will be required to submit a reflection of their learning.

33. Parents/ Guardians are strongly encouraged to play an active role in checking in with your child/ward on the selected
SIL activity and to motivate and support your child/ward to give his or her best in the pursuit of the activity.

**School Expectations**

36. Regular attendance is integral to student learning and engagement in school. Attendance in school is important to
ensure that all students gain knowledge and skills consistently throughout the school term. Being present in school
regularly will also increase the opportunities for positive relationship-building amongst students and between
teachers and students. Absences for overseas travel during the school term are strongly discouraged. We
encourage you to consider alternative arrangements where possible, so as not to disrupt your child’s school routine
and learning progress.

37. A medical certificate, from a medical practitioner registered under the Medical Registration Act on the Singapore
Medical Council website, is required if your child/ward is unwell and unable to attend school. A parent’s letter may
be provided for other types of valid absences, e.g. bereavement. **Permission must be sought from a School Leader via Form Teachers for all other absences, including overseas travel, during the school term.**

38. Parents/guardians who have onboarded PG will receive a notification via PG if student is absent from school. Kindly
submit your child’s/ward’s reason for absence after receiving the notification .

39. Students coming in 7.40am will be regarded as late for school and will face consequences (e.g. serving detention).
We encourage students to arrive in school at least five minutes before the respective reporting time. This will help
them frame their mind and prepare themselves to learn for the rest of the day. Latecomers’ mobile phones will be
kept in the general office until curriculum ends so that students can go to class promptly after admin procedures.

40. As a school, we strive to inculcate appropriate and responsible device usage in our students. In alignment with
MOE’s November 2025 guidelines, students are expected not to use handphones during school hours, including
recess and co-curricular activities. Handphones are to be kept in lockers or bags while students are in school.
Parents/Guardians who need to contact their child/ward urgently may do so via the General Office at 6385 1589.
Students who do not comply with the school’s expectations on electroni c device use may be subject to
consequences, including confiscation of the device.

41. The upkeep of our school facilities is crucial to ensure that our ongoing refurbishment efforts provide a conducive

property seriously and will take necessary actions to recover the costs of repairs or replacement. For example, the

**Safety and Security**

45. To ensure a safe and smooth commute to school, all student cyclists are required to register with our Operations
Manager. When cycling to school, students must adhere to road safety guidelines and are strongly encouraged to
wear cycling helmets, even if the students are using park connectors and pedestrian pavements for their entire
commute. As part of our commitment to student safety, we have a strict policy of not allowing bicycles without
functional brakes and personal mobility devices (PMDs) within the school compound.

46. To maintain a safe and secure school environment, we require all visitors to follow these steps:
a. Register at the Guard Room and obtain a visitor's pass from the security personnel.
b. Proceed to the General Office.
c. Visitors are not permitted to proceed to other parts of the school, including the classrooms.
d. Please remain at the school foyer or General Office unless accompanied by a school staff.

47. There are very limited parking spaces available within the school compound, hence, we kindly seek your
understanding to park at nearby HDB carparks when attending school events and programmes. The nearest carpark
is located at Block 475, Upper Serangoon Crescent. Please use the pedestrian crossing or the overhead bridge to
cross safely from the carpark to the school premises.
